## Article

The top of the banana bread looks ready for a photograph. The bottom has decided to stay in the pan. You tip it, wait, give it a hopeful shake — and suddenly you're holding a loaf with a piece missing. For the next batch, prepare a way to lift the bread before the batter goes in.

### Where is the loaf sticking?

Check the pan once you've removed what you can. A clean rim with a patch of bread left on the bottom suggests the base needs more release help. Bits along the sides are a reminder to check the exposed corners when preparing the next pan. Sticking doesn't, by itself, tell you whether the recipe was baked through. Follow its doneness checks as well.

A parchment sling is useful here. It lines the base and leaves paper tabs you can hold when it's time to remove the loaf. The tabs give you more control than tipping the pan and hoping the bread will come out whole.

### Fit the paper to your loaf pan

Set a strip across the pan's width so it covers the bottom, runs up both long sides, and leaves short handles above the rim. Fold the paper where the base meets the sides. Press it into place so a curved corner doesn't steal room from the batter.

To estimate the length of the strip, add the inside width, twice the depth, and a little extra for handles. For a pan 5 inches wide and 2¾ inches deep, with 1½-inch handles on each side, that is 5 + 5½ + 3 = 13½ inches. Cut the other dimension to fit along the pan's length. Your pan may differ, so measure inside it.

Keep the handles tidy and follow the parchment manufacturer's oven-use instructions. The paper should give you something to grip after baking while fitting neatly around the loaf. [King Arthur Baking's parchment-sling guide](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2017/03/27/parchment-paper-sling)

### Do you grease the pan, the paper, or both?

Use the recipe's preparation instructions. When it calls for greasing the pan, lining it, and greasing the parchment, give each step its turn. The first layer can help the paper stay put as you add batter, and the second helps the baked loaf separate from the liner. Spread the batter into the corners with the sling flat against the base. [Why some recipes grease parchment](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2025/02/27/do-you-grease-parchment-paper)

### Wait for the recipe's release point

Follow the cooling time your recipe gives before removing the loaf. A delicate quick bread needs a little time to hold together. Loosen any exposed edges with a utensil suitable for your pan's finish, then support the loaf as you lift the paper handles. Finish cooling on a rack before slicing or wrapping.

Write the pan preparation and cooling time beside your recipe. If you change the pan or liner next time, that small note helps you repeat what worked.
