## Article

The cookie tin is due Saturday. Thursday is already busy, and Friday night is no time to find out that a frozen brick of cookie dough takes forever to portion. Give each dough the shape it needs before it goes in the freezer. Then your baking day can start with a labeled bag and a cold sheet pan.

| Cookie style | Freeze it as | Before baking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chocolate chip or another drop cookie | Individual scoops | Bake from frozen when the recipe gives directions for it |
| Slice-and-bake shortbread | A wrapped log | Thaw in the refrigerator until firm enough to slice cleanly |
| Rolled sugar-cookie cutouts | A flat, wrapped disk | Thaw in the refrigerator until pliable, then roll and cut |

### Scoop drop cookies before you freeze them

Follow any resting or chilling step in the recipe. Portion the dough and set the scoops close together on a parchment-lined tray, with room between them. Freeze until firm, then transfer the portions to a freezer bag. Write the oven temperature, the portion size, and the recipe's baking directions on the label. If the recipe calls for pressing the dough balls flat, do that before freezing when the recipe gives that instruction.

Check the recipe for baking from frozen. King Arthur's chocolate sourdough discard cookies call for two extra minutes, while its monster cookies can bake straight from frozen as directed in that recipe. If your recipe gives no frozen timing, bake two test cookies first. Check the edges and centers against the recipe's doneness cues and note the timing on the bag before committing the full tray. [Chocolate sourdough discard cookies](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/chocolate-sourdough-discard-cookies-recipe) · [Monster cookies](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/monster-cookies-recipe)

### Freeze shortbread logs and cutout dough in the shape you'll use

For slice-and-bake cookies, wrap the log snugly so its surface stays protected. Let it soften in the refrigerator until a knife can make an even slice. Wrap cutout dough as a flat disk so it can thaw evenly before rolling. Once the dough is pliable, cut the shapes and follow the recipe's chilling instructions before baking. If the recipe already gives directions for freezing shaped cutouts, use those. [King Arthur Baking's make-ahead cookie guide](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2021/11/26/can-you-make-cookie-dough-ahead-of-time)

### Which cookie scoop size should you buy?

Start with the portion called for in the recipe. “Medium” can mean different things across brands. Check the stated capacity in tablespoons, or use a kitchen scale if your recipe gives weights. A level measuring spoon works for occasional batches. A squeeze-release scoop earns its drawer space if you make several trays at a time and want to portion them quickly. OXO identifies its medium Good Grips model as a #40 portioner and lists it as dishwasher-safe. Choose it if its stated capacity matches the cookies you bake most often. [OXO specifications](https://www.oxo.com/medium-cookie-scoop-661.html) · [King Arthur Baking's scoop-size trial](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2025/09/25/cookie-scoop-sizes)

### Make the cookie-tin deadline easier

If the cookies need icing, leave time to decorate, let them set, and pack them. If you expect the oven to be busy on the day you need the tin, bake the cookies ahead, let them cool completely, and freeze them well wrapped if the recipe supports freezing baked cookies. Leave the fancy decorating for after thawing where the recipe allows it.
