## Article

At 4:45 on Thanksgiving, the turkey is resting, the oven is full, and somebody has just asked when the rolls are going in. If the answer is “once they finish rising,” the breadbasket may be the last thing on the table. Start with the oven time you can actually spare. Then choose which part of the roll recipe to do ahead.

| Your Thanksgiving kitchen | What to do ahead | What happens on the day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The oven schedule is packed | Bake your usual rolls all the way, cool, and freeze | Thaw, then warm briefly if the oven frees up |
| You can reserve a short, hot oven slot | Follow a recipe developed for brown-and-serve rolls | Finish browning the rolls straight from the freezer |
| You have counter time and a full baking slot | Follow a recipe or tested method for freezing shaped yeast dough | Allow time to thaw and rise before baking |

### Already have a favorite recipe? Bake it before the holiday

Make plain rolls as you normally would and cool them completely. Pack them airtight in a freezer bag or a tightly sealed freezer-safe container. A dozen rolls may be easier to handle as two packs of six, especially if you want to save some for leftovers. Label the package with the date and any finishing step, such as “brush with butter after warming.”

On the day, thaw the rolls at room temperature in their wrapping. If you want them warm, transfer them to an oven-safe dish and cover loosely with foil while you warm them gently. Add a finishing brush of butter just before they go to the table. If oven space disappears, the thawed rolls are already baked and ready to serve. [King Arthur Baking's guide to freezing and defrosting bread](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/12/20/freeze-bread)

### When brown-and-serve rolls earn their oven space

If you can reserve a hot oven slot, a brown-and-serve recipe gives you that fresh golden finish right before dinner. Bake the first stage as directed, cool completely, then freeze and package the rolls according to the recipe. On Thanksgiving, take out the number you need and finish them from frozen. King Arthur's recipe calls for 8–12 minutes at 425°F for individually separated rolls, with extra time for rolls frozen as a connected slab. That timing applies to that recipe. Block out preheating time too, especially if the oven is coming from a lower temperature. [Homemade Brown and Serve Rolls recipe](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/homemade-brown-and-serve-rolls-recipe)

### Can you freeze the dough before baking?

Yes, when the recipe or method is designed for it. Shaped rolls still need time to thaw and rise before they bake. If you're serving at 5 p.m., work backward from the recipe's bake time and start the thaw with hours to spare. Follow the recipe's visual proofing cues, since a cool kitchen can slow the rise. A home baker asking about King Arthur's quick rolls raised this exact scheduling question, and King Arthur's tested freeze-and-bake method is a better reference for timing than a holiday dinner countdown. [Freeze and bake rolls](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2015/10/05/freeze-bake-rolls)

### Put the finishing instructions on the freezer bag

Write down three things: how many rolls are inside, whether they're fully baked or parbaked, and what has to happen before serving. If someone else is taking over the bread while you make gravy, they can tell whether the rolls need warming or still need their final bake. Use freezer-rated packaging that closes without squashing the rolls. If you're freezing them on a sheet first, check that the sheet fits flat in both the freezer and the oven.
