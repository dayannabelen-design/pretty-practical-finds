Why bread needs to cool before slicing—and what to do while you wait

Why the pause matters

King Arthur recommends cooling bread completely before cutting to help avoid a gummy interior. A browned crust is not a promise that the inside is ready for neat slices. Build cooling time into the bake rather than treating the oven timer as the moment lunch is served.

Set up the cooling spot before the loaf comes out

  • Clear enough counter space for the rack and the pan.
  • Choose a level rack that supports the loaf without wobbling.
  • Follow the recipe’s directions for when to turn the bread out.
  • Keep room for air beneath the loaf.

How long should you wait?

Use your recipe’s guidance and let the loaf cool fully. Size and shape vary, so one universal countdown would be misleading. A roll and a large sandwich loaf are different cooling jobs.

If you need slices at a particular time, bake earlier or choose a baking window with some margin. That is easier than discovering the conflict with a knife already in your hand.

What makes a rack useful?

The slicing setup

  1. Let the loaf cool, then move it to a stable board.
  2. Leave space beside it for the slices.
  3. Use a serrated bread knife with a gentle sawing motion, keeping fingers clear.
  4. Move finished slices aside before the board becomes crowded.
  5. Package the portion for later or freeze it in useful amounts.

One small job while you wait

Put away the flour and mark any useful notes on the recipe: the pan you used, the dough weight or the step you want to improve. By the time you return to the loaf, the next part of baking day will have somewhere to happen.
