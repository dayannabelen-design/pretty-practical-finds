Banana bread in grams: a cozy 9×5-inch loaf

9×5-inch pan · 325°F · About 70 minutes baking

Makes: 1 loaf

Ingredients

  • 113 g unsalted butter, cool room temperature
  • 142 g packed brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon
  • ¼ teaspoon nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon table salt
  • 340 g mashed banana
  • 64 g apricot jam or orange marmalade (optional)
  • 85 g honey
  • 2 large eggs
  • 270 g all-purpose flour
  • 57 g walnuts (optional)

Method

  1. Grease a 9×5-inch pan. Heat oven to 325°F.
  2. Beat butter, sugar, vanilla, spices, baking soda, baking powder and salt until smooth.
  3. Mix in banana, optional jam, honey and eggs. Stir in flour and optional walnuts just until combined.
  4. Fill pan; rest 10 minutes. Bake 45 minutes, cover loosely with foil, then bake about 25 minutes more. A center tester should show crumbs, not wet batter; bake longer if needed.
  5. Cool in pan 10 minutes; turn onto a rack and cool completely.

Source: PJ Hamel — Banana Bread. Visit the original for extended notes and variations.

Give the bananas their own little weighing ceremony

Set a bowl on the scale before you begin, then zero it. Peel, mash and weigh the fruit into that bowl. Write the weight on a scrap of paper. It is a small kindness to your future self, who may be answering a question from the next room while trying to remember whether that last banana went in.

For a calmer setup, put the ingredients on one side of the bowl and move each to the other side after adding it. The cinnamon jar becomes a tiny progress marker. Our guide to weighing bread ingredients explains the tare button.

Keep a loaf notebook

Record your pan’s inside dimensions, the time you first checked the center, and when you took the loaf out. A photograph of the cut center is more useful than a vague “turned out nice.” Next time, you will have notes from your own oven rather than another tab to open.

If your pan is a different size, read the loaf-pan comparison before pouring batter. Save the last page of your notebook for the slices you would bake again.
