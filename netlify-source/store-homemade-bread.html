How to store homemade bread when one loaf lasts more than a day

The first slice rarely needs a plan. The rest of the loaf does. Divide it while it is fresh and completely cool, and tomorrow’s toast becomes a much easier decision.

The useful bit, first

Let the loaf cool completely. Keep the portion you will eat soon at room temperature using suitable wrapping, and freeze the rest in portions you can use.

A freezer routine you can repeat

  1. Cool the loaf completely before slicing or packaging.
  2. Set aside the amount you expect to eat soon.
  3. Slice the freezer portion if you want to remove a little at a time.
  4. Separate portions with parchment if the slices tend to stick.
  5. Package airtight, label with the name and date, and freeze.

Portion for breakfast, not for the photograph

If you normally use two slices at a time, make that your starting portion. If a whole family eats together, a larger packet may be more convenient. A single frozen block is less helpful when everyone wants bread at a different time.

King Arthur’s storage guide recommends slicing sourdough before freezing for flexibility. The useful detail is access: you can take what you need without handling the whole loaf.

What the bag needs to do

The Ziploc freezer bags linked below are an option for cooled bread portions. Check the usable size against your slices. If you already have suitable freezer packaging, try the routine with that first; a new bag cannot compensate for packaging the bread while it is still warm.

Do you need a bread box as well?

Only if it solves a storage problem in your kitchen. Check the opening, the height of your loaf and the counter space it will occupy. A box does not remove the need to choose suitable wrapping or decide when to freeze the extra bread.
