The recipe card

Evaporated milk sandwich bread: three loaves for a slower Sunday

Three 8½×4½-inch pans · 375°F · About 35 minutes baking

Makes: 3 loaves

Ingredients

  • 454 g lukewarm water
  • 142 g evaporated milk
  • 71 g softened butter
  • 50 g granulated sugar
  • 18 g table salt
  • 11 g instant yeast or active dry yeast
  • 840–960 g all-purpose flour

Method

  1. Mix water, milk, sugar and yeast. Add butter, salt and 840 g flour; mix, then knead until smooth and soft, adding flour as needed within the listed range.
  2. Cover in a greased bowl; let rise until puffy, about 1–1½ hours.
  3. Grease three 8½×4½-inch pans. Divide dough into six equal pieces; round and place two side by side in each pan.
  4. Cover; rise about 45 minutes until puffy. Heat oven to 375°F.
  5. Bake about 35 minutes until golden and the center reaches 190°F. Unmold onto a rack; cool.

Source: King Arthur Test Kitchen — Our Favorite Big-Batch Sandwich Bread. Visit the original for extended notes and variations.

A little planning before the flour cloud

Clear the cooling space before you start mixing. Three finished loaves are an awkward surprise when every inch of counter is occupied by breakfast dishes. Set out the pans, make room for a rack, and choose a place for your recipe where a stray splash cannot reach it.

Check your mixer’s dough-capacity guidance before loading a large batch. Bowl volume alone does not tell you what its motor can knead. A small bread-tool kit can be useful without turning the counter into an equipment showroom.

The loaf that belongs to later

Decide which loaf is for today before everyone starts negotiating for the end pieces. Label a freezer bag while your hands are clean, then set it aside for packaging after cooling. Put the date and what you intend to use the bread for on the label: toast, lunch sandwiches, a rainy-day grilled cheese.
